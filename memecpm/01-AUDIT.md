# memecpm.com — audit of the current page (29 Sep 2026)

Based on the page text Evan pasted (saved at `context/sites/memecpm/index.md`). Screenshots and the tech
stack will be added to `context/sites/memecpm/NOTES.md` when a capture session can reach the site.

## What the page is today

A single landing page selling **AI-produced short-form video** to DTC and Amazon brands:
"Content" at $1,000/month (40 videos) and "Content + Distribution" at $1,500/month, with a $200 pilot
(5 videos in 7 days) that credits against month one. Proof is a Levanta dashboard showing $10,453 in
tracked Amazon sales and 299 orders from organic videos, plus screenshots of reels with big save counts.

## What's genuinely strong (keep all of this)

1. **The proof block.** "$10,453 tracked · 299 orders · no ad spend · source: Levanta" is rare. Most
   agencies show view counts; this shows *orders*. It's the most valuable thing on the site.
2. **The pilot mechanic.** $200, 7 days, credited to month one. Low-risk, concrete, easy to say yes to.
3. **The guarantees.** On time or refund, 3 rounds of changes, deposit not fee. Keep verbatim.
4. **"How a month runs."** Four steps, plain language. Buyers want to know what happens after they pay.
5. **The distribution story.** "Pages I built and run myself… 1.3M views in week one… 8K saves on one
   reel." A real network is a moat; most clipping agencies don't own any pages.
6. **Founder voice.** First person, direct, no agency-speak. Keep it.
7. **Disclosure line.** "All videos on this page are AI-generated" and paid-post labelling. Keep; it's
   the right thing and it pre-empts the objection.

## What's holding it back

1. **The headline sells the method, not the outcome.** "Short-form AI video" leads with *how* the
   videos are made, which is the thing Evan is least proud of (quality) and the thing buyers are most
   wary of. The outcome ("$10k of tracked sales from organic short-form") is buried in the second line.
2. **AI video is a shrinking edge.** Every brand can now generate an AI talking head for $30/month.
   Selling the production alone is a race to the bottom. Selling *volume + distribution + proof* isn't.
3. **The name says CPM, the page never mentions views-based pricing.** "memecpm" promises pay-per-view
   economics. There's no CPM offer on the page, so the domain and the offer don't match.
4. **40 videos/month is low for the price point** once the product becomes clips. Freelancers charge
   $15–60 per clip; an automated pipeline can deliver 100–300 clips for the same money and that volume
   is the real differentiator.
5. **No "what a clip looks like."** The work samples are AI videos. For a clipping offer the sample
   should be a before/after: a 40-minute source video → 30 posting-ready clips in a Drive folder with a
   tracker. Show the deliverable, not just the video.
6. **One CTA type: "Book a call."** Founders of small DTC brands often won't book a call for a $200
   pilot. Add a no-call path: fill a short form, pay the pilot, send footage.
7. **No FAQ.** Objections (rights to the footage, what counts as a "clip", who posts, what if I have no
   footage, what about #ad labels) go unanswered until the call.
8. **No urgency or capacity signal.** "Founding-client rates for my first brands" is good; it needs a
   number ("taking 5 brands this quarter") to work.
9. **Contact block puts a personal phone number on a public page.** Fine if intended; a form + Discord
   + email is enough and stops spam calls.

## The pivot, in one line

**From:** "I make AI videos for your brand."
**To:** "Send me one video. I turn it into a hundred clips, post them across a network that has already
sold $10k of product, and you pay for what gets seen."

The proof, the pilot, the guarantees and the distribution story all carry over unchanged. Only the
production method and the volume change, and both get better.

## Where the clips come from (so the page is honest)

- **Client footage first.** Founder videos, product demos, UGC the brand already owns, podcast/YouTube
  content, TikTok Shop affiliate videos they have rights to, livestreams. Real footage, real people,
  no AI-quality concern.
- **AI videos as an add-on**, for brands with no footage at all, priced per video. This keeps the current
  product alive without leading with it.
- **Production runs on the cloud clip pipeline** (transcribe → find the moments → cut → 9:16 reframe with
  face tracking → captions → hook text → music bed → variants → QC → Drive bank + tracker). The same
  engine that produced the 680-video Katie bank. Fallback: the desktop clip maker.

## Numbers from the market (for pricing decisions)

- Freelance clippers: **$15–60 per clip**, driven by turnaround.
- AI clipping SaaS (DIY): **$19–99/month**, but the brand still has to post and has no distribution.
- Done-for-you agencies: **$1,500–8,000/month** retainers.
- Managed clipping campaigns billed on views: **$0.02–0.10 CPM at the bulk low end; $1–5 CPM on
  Whop-style creator networks** (platform average about $1.25 paid to clippers).
- Clippers on Whop/Content Rewards are paid **$1–5 per 1,000 verified views**; top campaigns fill fast.
- Paid social CPMs for comparison: **$10–30+**.
